Emma Verweij is an interdisciplinary PhD researcher in the Land Use Planning (LUP) and Environmental Economics and Natural Resources (ENR) groups at Wageningen University. Together with her supervisors, Andries Richter (ENR), Francesco Orsi (LUP), and Martha Bakker (LUP), she studies the effects of a phosphate rights trading market: an environmental policy introduced for dairy farmers in the Netherlands to control manure production and thereby reduce nutrient pollution.

She approaches the agricultural system as a social-ecological system and uses agent-based modeling as her main method. This allows her to capture dairy farmers' decision-making and to assess how different policy design options, such as the share of rights skimmed off during trade and the spatial allocation of rights, affect policy outcomes such as the land use intensity of dairy farming (livestock units per hectare). The overall aim of her research is to identify how this cap-and-trade policy can enable both more intensive and more extensive forms of dairy farming, while minimizing societal costs. Supporting extensification and a shift toward more nature-inclusive dairy farming, especially in areas where intensive dairy farming is no longer desirable for environmental reasons, contributes to one of the objectives of NL2120, the program that funds her research.
