I am an interdisciplinary PhD researcher in the Land Use Planning (LUP) and Environmental Economics and Natural Resources (ENR) groups at Wageningen University. Together with my supervisors, Andries Richter (ENR) and Francesco Orsi and Martha Bakker (LUP), I study the effects of a phosphate rights trading market: an environmental policy introduced for dairy farmers in the Netherlands to control manure production and thereby reduce nutrient pollution.

I approach the agricultural system as a social-ecological system and use agent-based modelling as my main method. This allows me to capture dairy farmers' decision-making and to assess how different policy design options, such as the share of rights skimmed off during trade and the spatial allocation of rights, affect policy outcomes such as the land use intensity of dairy farming (livestock units per hectare). The overall aim of my research is to identify how this cap-and-trade policy can enable both more intensive and more extensive forms of dairy farming, while minimising societal costs. Supporting extensification and a shift towards more nature-inclusive dairy farming, especially in areas where intensive dairy farming is no longer desirable for environmental reasons, contributes to one of the objectives of NL2120, the programme that funds my research.
